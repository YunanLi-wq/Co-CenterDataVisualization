National Diet and Nutrition Survey (NDNS) Report — Northern Ireland

The National Diet and Nutrition Survey (NDNS) Report for Northern Ireland

Research project — Northern Ireland specific. The NDNS RPORG: Results for Years 5 to 9 (2012/13 - 2016/17) (combined) and time trend and income analysis Years 1 to 9 (2008/09 - 2016/17).

The NDNS RPORG is a continuous cross-sectional survey, designed to assess the diet, nutrient intake and nutritional status of the general population aged 1.5 years and over living in private households in the UK.

Boosted samples were gathered in Years 1-4 (2008/09-2011/12) and Years 6-9 (2013/14-2016/17) to achieve representative data for Northern Ireland and enable comparisons with UK results.

Key findings

Consumption of 5 A Day fruit and vegetable portions was below the recommendation in all age/sex groups.

Average consumption of oily fish was equivalent to 7-15g per week in children and 30-60g per week in adults, below the recommended 1 portion (140g) per week.

Average daily consumption of red and processed meat for men aged 19 to 64 years exceeded the current maximum recommendation by 19g.

Average intakes of saturated fatty acids exceeded the current recommendation of no more than 11% of food energy, providing 12.7-13.3% of food energy for children aged 4 years upwards and 13.3-14.0% for adults.

Intakes of free sugars exceeded the recommendation of no more than 5% of total energy from free sugars in all age/sex groups.

For AOAC fibre, average intakes were 9.9g for children aged 1.5 to 3 years, 12.9g for ages 4 to 10 years, and 14.6g for children aged 11 to 18 years. Intakes for adults were 17.3-17.6g.

Average intakes of vitamin D were below the recommended 10µg/day in all age/sex groups.

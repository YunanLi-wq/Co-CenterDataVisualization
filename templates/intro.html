Food Co-Centre Sustainability Compass

What is the Food Co-Centre Sustainability Compass?

The Food Co-Centre Sustainability Compass displays food systems outcomes to consider in Ireland and the UK. It identifies four major societal goals (quadrants), each of which is divided into a number of areas of concern (segments). These are then sub-divided into food system issues (factors). The fourth ring of the Compass is food system outcome data and metrics (variables). Click on 'Data Search' to search the data and metrics collected to date.

Why a Sustainability Compass?

The current state of the food system requires transformation. However, researchers and policymakers typically work in silos and fail to comprehend the entirety of the food system and how a proposed intervention may result in synergies (benefits) as well as trade-offs (negative unintended consequences). The Compass allows users to see an overview of all food system outcomes to consider at an Irish and UK level.

What are the purposes of the Food Co-Centre Sustainability Compass?

There are six purposes of the Food Co-Centre Sustainability Compass:

  1. To collate the status of key variables and metrics for food system outcomes.
  2. To assess the impact of Co-Centre innovations related to individual quadrants.
  3. To highlight the synergies and trade-offs of Co-Centre innovations across all the quadrants.
  4. To help develop coordinated government and private sector policy (Co-Centre vision).
  5. To map research effort across the Co-Centre programme (gap analysis).
  6. To facilitate citizen engagement.

How was the Compass developed?

As part of the Co-Centre for Sustainable Food Systems, a transdisciplinary participatory design approach with Irish and UK food system stakeholders was undertaken to co-design the Compass. The wording and structure of the Compass is a result of a multi-step iterative design and development process involving stakeholders working in the public sector, industry, non-governmental organisations as well as academia.
